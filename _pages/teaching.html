TEACHING & MENTORING

Evidence first.
Student ownership always.

I teach Earth and environmental science as a way of reasoning: ask a defensible question, evaluate observations, recognize uncertainty, and explain how the evidence supports a conclusion.

Learning by doing
the work of science.

More than a decade of teaching experience,
with students from geology, biology,
engineering, and oceanography.

01 / INQUIRY

Predict. Observe. Interpret.

I ask students to articulate expectations before collecting data, then compare their predictions with what they observe. Unexpected results become opportunities to distinguish uncertainty, natural variability, and competing explanations.

02 / PARTICIPATION

Make rigorous work accessible.

Clear expectations, low-stakes practice, transparent rubrics, and multiple forms of participation help students with different backgrounds build confidence and analytical skills.

03 / CONNECTION

Bring field data back to class.

Field and laboratory observations become material for mapping, visualization, data analysis, and scientific writing. Students connect methods with the questions those methods can answer.

RESEARCH MENTORING

Progressive independence.
Shared discovery.

I have advised or co-advised 18 students and research technicians, including 11 undergraduates. Their work spans paleolimnology, lake monitoring, experimental ecology, and environmental observatory data.

Regular meetings, written milestones, shared protocols, and increasing responsibility help students develop their own scientific judgment and contribute to publications, presentations, and research products.
